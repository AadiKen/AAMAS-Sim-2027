# Ship SQCM method reconstruction and implementation gates

Primary source: F. A. Ayubi, *Influence of Hydrodynamic Derivatives on Ship Manoeuvring Prediction and Application of SQCM to Ship Hull Forces*, Kyushu University doctoral thesis (2021), [full text](https://catalog.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/opac_download_md/4496053/eng3141.pdf), Chapters 4–5. The earlier [Wigley article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/344975143_Prediction_of_Hydrodynamic_Forces_Acting_on_Wigley_Hull_based_on_SQCM) presents the same ship formulation. The [trimaran application](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0029801824011508) reports captive-force-derived derivatives and MMG trajectories, but its accessible abstract does not specify a turning-flow SQCM boundary condition or provide numerical force tables. Nothing below treats ordinary aircraft VLM as equivalent to SQCM.

| Hull surface | Port and starboard wetted hull plus their waterline mirror: a double-body quadrilateral source-panel surface. Each panel has one constant source unknown, a collocation point and outward normal. | Thesis §4.3, pp. 61–71, eqs. 4.2–4.27; §5.2.2, pp. 88–89 |
| Hull discretization | Longitudinal and vertical structured panels; Wigley uses 30 × 5 per side and a mirror (600 source panels). KCS/KVLCC2 use the same selected grid. | Thesis §5.2.2, pp. 88–89; §5.3.2, pp. 96–99 |
| Source influence | Hess–Smith constant-source quadrilateral influence, transformed into each panel's local coordinates. Self and edge singular limits require explicit treatment. All source-panel velocities enter both hull and vortex-plane boundary rows. | Thesis §4.3.1–4.3.4, pp. 61–71, eqs. 4.2–4.27 |
| Vortex representation | Quasi-continuous horseshoe vortices on each hull's **central longitudinal plane**. Chordwise positions/weights use Lan's altered trapezoidal/Chebyshev construction; the strength tends to zero at the trailing edge, which enforces the Kutta condition without an extra arbitrary trailing-edge equation. | Thesis §4.2, p. 59–61; §4.4, pp. 71–75, eqs. 4.28–4.36 |
| Vortex influence | Finite-segment Biot–Savart velocities from the bound spanwise segment and both free legs, including contributions at source collocation and vortex weighting points. | Thesis §4.4.2–4.4.3, pp. 72–75, eqs. 4.32–4.36 |
| Coupled solve | Solve **source and circulation strengths simultaneously**: at every hull and central-plane control point, `(V0 + Vs + Vv)·n = 0`. The complete block matrix has `M+N` rows and unknowns. | Thesis §4.6, pp. 76–77, eqs. 4.37–4.40 |
| Wake Model 1 | Both free legs of all central-plane horseshoes originate at the stern. | Thesis §4.5, p. 75, Fig. 4.10 |
| Wake Model 2 | Free legs of bottom-region horseshoes originate from the bottom; remaining legs leave the stern. This materially changes bottom and side pressure. | Thesis §4.5, p. 75, Fig. 4.10; §5.2.3–5.2.4, pp. 89–93 |
| Wake length and initial direction | The selected runs use 50 segments per free leg and a prescribed initial shedding angle (often equal to the drift angle). A 70-segment KCS/KVLCC2 calculation roughly doubled runtime without clear force improvement. This is a convergence observation, not a universal default. | Thesis §5.2.2, pp. 88–89; §5.3.2, pp. 96–99 |
| Wake deformation | After each coupled solution, convect each free-vortex node using uniform + source + vortex velocity times `Δt`; add a node when the gap at its root exceeds nominal element length, then rebuild influences and re-solve until Y/N approximately settle. The publication uses 100 iterations in its selected studies. | Thesis §4.7–4.8, pp. 77–82, eqs. 4.41–4.43 |
| Oblique flow | Solve the boundary condition with the full incoming vector at drift angle β. The Wigley study used U = 0.5 m/s; do not infer a measured force from an unreadable graph. | Thesis §5.2.2–5.2.3, pp. 88–91 |
| Turning flow | A mathematically consistent body-frame boundary velocity would vary with point: `V∞(x,y)=-(u,v,0)-ω×(x,y,z)`. **This is an extension**, not a published, numerically qualified turning SQCM formula in the accessible thesis. The trimaran paper reports MMG turning trajectories using SQCM-derived coefficients, which does not prove a pure-yaw captive SQCM solution. | Thesis Chapter 5 scope; Gong et al. (2024) abstract |
| Pressure and load | Surface `Cp=1-|V|²/U²`; integrate `dF=-½ρU² Cp n dS` and `dN=(x-x_ref)dF_y-(y-y_ref)dF_x` in BCOD FRD. This is the physical fluid-on-hull load; BCOD's resisting wrench has opposite sign. | Thesis §4.9, pp. 82–84, eqs. 4.44–4.46; BCOD frame contract |
| Nondimensionalization | Thesis `Cy=Fy/(½ρ S U²)`, `Cm=Mz/(½ρ L² d U²)`. These are **not** automatically HMRI's coefficient normalization. Convert dimensional loads before any locked HMRI fit. | Thesis §4.9, p. 83–84, eq. 4.46 |
| Free surface and limits | Double-body approximation has no wave-making/free-surface dynamics. Authors find Model 2 better than Model 1 for small drift on Wigley/KCS/KVLCC2, but still substantial yaw-moment and large-drift discrepancy. | Thesis §§5.2–5.4, pp. 85–109; Chapter 6 |

## Qualification before HMRI

The experimentally frozen `bcod-passive-phase3a-1.0.0` remains separate. An SQCM candidate requires: preserved structured geometry; convergent sphere/ellipsoid source panel tests; independently signed vortex/Kutta tests; low coupled hull-normal and central-plane residuals; convergent wake Models 1/2; quantitative published Wigley and ship reproduction; then a code/config freeze **before** the locked HMRI campaign. A source-only panel solver, a generic vortex lattice, or the thesis' graphs without digitized numerical values is insufficient evidence to label a result SQCM or claim a hybrid improvement.
